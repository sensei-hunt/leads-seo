Understanding Your Flexible Spending Account (FSA)

A Flexible Spending Account (FSA) is one of the most valuable employee benefits available through Alliance Insurance Group. Authorized under Section 125 of the Internal Revenue Code (also called a "cafeteria plan"), an FSA allows you to set aside a portion of your paycheck before taxes to pay for eligible healthcare expenses.

How an FSA Saves You Money

When you contribute to an FSA, the money is deducted from your paycheck before federal income tax, Social Security tax (FICA), and Medicare tax are calculated. This means every dollar you put into your FSA reduces your taxable income. For example, if you contribute $2,000 to your FSA and are in the 22% federal tax bracket, you could save approximately $600 or more in taxes annually.

Types of FSA Accounts

  • Healthcare FSA – Covers eligible medical, dental, and vision expenses for you, your spouse, and dependents. Includes prescriptions, copays, over-the-counter medications, medical equipment, and hundreds of health products.
  • Dependent Care FSA (DCFSA) – Covers eligible childcare and eldercare expenses such as daycare, preschool, before/after school care, summer day camp, and adult dependent care for qualifying dependents.
  • Limited Purpose FSA (LPFSA) – Available to employees with an HSA. Covers dental and vision expenses only until the deductible is met.

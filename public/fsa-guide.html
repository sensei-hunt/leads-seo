Understanding Your Flexible Spending Account (FSA)

A Flexible Spending Account (FSA) is one of the most powerful tools available to employees for reducing healthcare costs. By setting aside pre-tax dollars from your paycheck, you can pay for eligible medical, dental, and vision expenses while lowering your overall tax burden. Alliance Insurance Group makes it easy to enroll, manage, and maximize your FSA benefits.

How Does an FSA Work?

During your employer's open enrollment period, you choose how much to contribute to your FSA for the plan year. Your contributions are deducted from each paycheck before federal income tax, Social Security tax, and most state income taxes are calculated. This means every dollar you put into your FSA gives you more purchasing power than an after-tax dollar.

For example, if you're in the 22% federal tax bracket and contribute $2,850 to your FSA, you could save approximately $627 in federal income tax alone — not including Social Security and state tax savings.

What Expenses Are Eligible?

FSA funds can be used for a wide range of healthcare expenses, including:

  • Doctor visit copays and deductibles
  • Prescription medications
  • Dental treatments including cleanings, fillings, and orthodontia
  • Vision care including eye exams, glasses, and contact lenses
  • Over-the-counter medications (with or without a prescription, per IRS rules)
  • Medical equipment such as blood pressure monitors and first aid supplies
  • Mental health services and therapy sessions
  • Sunscreen and other preventive care items

Contribution Limits for 2026

The IRS sets annual contribution limits for FSAs. For the 2026 plan year, the maximum employee contribution is $3,050 for a Healthcare FSA. Some employers also offer a Dependent Care FSA with a separate contribution limit of up to $5,000 per household for child or elder care expenses.

The Use-It-or-Lose-It Rule

FSA funds generally must be used within the plan year. However, many employers — including those partnered with Alliance Insurance Group — offer one of two safety nets: a grace period of up to 2.5 extra months to spend remaining funds, or a carryover provision allowing up to $610 to roll into the next plan year. Check with your employer to see which option applies to your plan.

Tips to Maximize Your FSA

  • Review last year's medical expenses to estimate your annual healthcare spending
  • Schedule dental cleanings, eye exams, and preventive visits early in the plan year
  • Stock up on eligible over-the-counter items before the deadline
  • Use the Alliance Insurance Group FSA store to shop for eligible products online
  • Keep all receipts in case your plan administrator requests documentation
